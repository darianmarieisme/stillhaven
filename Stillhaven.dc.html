You do not have to be in danger to use any of these. Save your warmline while you're calm — that's the number hardest to find later.
I need to talk to someone
A warmline
Peer listening for distress, loneliness, or wanting to talk about anything — not an emergency line. Hours and coverage vary by area, so save yours while calm.
988 — emotional support, any reason (US)
Call, text, or chat, any hour. It is not "suicide only" — it covers emotional distress, relationship trouble, loneliness, substance concerns, or just needing someone to talk to.
I'm unsafe because of another person
These services offer confidential support, safety planning, information, and local referrals when harm is coming from another person. If anyone is in immediate physical danger or needs urgent medical help, use emergency services.
RAINN — National Sexual Assault Hotline
Free, confidential, 24/7 support for anyone affected by sexual violence, in English and en Español. Call 800.656.HOPE (4673), chat at rainn.org/hotline, or text HOPE to 64673.
National Domestic Violence Hotline
Live advocates, 24/7 — including for coercion, threats, and escalating conflict you're not sure how to name. Call 800.799.SAFE (7233), text START to 88788, or chat online.
Caution if you visit online: The Hotline warns that internet activity can be monitored and may not be completely erasable. If someone may have access to your device, calling or texting is safer.
I need practical help
United Way
Call or text 211 and they'll guide you toward local resources and support — food, housing, transportation, utility help, health services, and more. In the US, local 211 services advertise 24/7 access and support in up to 180 languages. In Canada, 211 is available in most of the country, 24 hours a day, in more than 150 languages.
911 — immediate physical danger
Use emergency services if you or someone else is in immediate danger, a medical emergency has occurred, or urgent in-person help is needed. A different function from wanting grounding and human contact — it sits outside your support list on purpose.

Add something from your own history
The suggestions above are only a starting point. For this plan to work well, add at least one specific urge or action you know you may struggle with when overwhelmed.
The suggestions above can't cover every way you respond when overwhelmed. Take a moment to think honestly about actions you've taken that were risky, hard to undo, or caused more pain afterward.
This is not about judging yourself. Naming a pattern gives you the chance to interrupt it. Be specific about the action rather than labelling yourself.
Instead of “I become toxic,” write “I send messages designed to hurt them.”
Instead of “I lose control,” write “I drive too fast when I'm angry.”
Instead of “I ruin relationships,” write “I threaten to leave when I actually want reassurance.”
Honesty here protects you later. The app can only interrupt the patterns you're willing to name.

One check before you continue
For some people with borderline personality disorder, a “favorite person” is someone they rely on heavily for comfort, reassurance, or validation. That person may feel like the obvious support contact, but they may also be involved in the conflict or panic you need help stepping back from.
Could this person sometimes be a source of crisis?
If so, it's strongly recommended to add another person or support option outside that relationship. This is not a judgment about the relationship. It is a backup plan for when that person is unavailable or involved.
